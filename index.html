TL;DR

A multiplayer world model must predict how the independent actions of several players change a shared environment, including changes outside a player's current field of view. A POV video observes such changes only after they enter the camera, whereas panoramic video records the surroundings in every viewing direction. No existing Minecraft dataset pairs synchronized POV and panoramic videos of interacting players with their action controls. PAWA contributes:

  • A dataset and a scalable, programmable pipeline. Each player's POV video is paired with a world-stabilized panorama and aligned actions. Gameplay capture runs on CPUs without graphics clients and scales beyond 64 simultaneous players. GPU rendering is separate, and each replay can be rendered again with relative depth or along custom camera trajectories without repeating the gameplay.
  • Geometry-aligned dual towers. A POV tower and a panorama tower jointly predict the two videos. Their cross-tower attention encodes world-frame viewing rays as rotary phases, which aligns the two projections by construction.
  • Quadratic-phase player encoding. The identity pool grows from m+1 to m2 codes at a fixed embedding dimension, with closed-form correlations between any two codes.

Experiments show that PAWA outperforms state-of-the-art multiplayer world models in POV prediction quality while producing high-fidelity panoramic predictions.

Panoramic multiplayer dataset

Training a panoramic multiplayer world model requires both projections and every player's actions from one synchronized interaction. Existing multiplayer data, such as the Solaris dataset, is recorded from live Minecraft clients, so each recording contains only the perspective view displayed during play. Our pipeline records programmable interactions on CPUs and renders the saved replays offline on GPUs. A single interaction then yields POV and panoramic videos, relative depth and custom camera trajectories without repeating the gameplay.

Scaling collection

SolarisEngine pairs each controller with a live graphics client, so both computation and rendering grow with the player count. PAWA removes rendering from gameplay. Its Rust player runtime simulates more than 64 simultaneous players and records per-tick controls, positions, camera orientations and player state. GPU workers render the saved replays later, so capture and rendering scale with separate resources.

Generating and validating interactions

Scenarios are written as natural-language descriptions of behaviors and expected outcomes. Coding agents compose reusable primitives into scripts. A script can run without error and still miss its interaction, so behavioral QC checks the recorded actions and a vision-language model reviews sampled renders. Failures return to the agents for revision.

Large sessions, observed from anywhere

Counts are connected, recorded participants. Terrain and other characters can occlude players. These recordings show collection capability, not model generation. The model is trained and evaluated on 2–16 players.

Observing a shared world beyond the local view

Panoramic multiplayer world modeling

Given the initial observations of every player in both projections, PAWA models

$$p_\theta\big(\{X^v_{p,1:T}\}_{p,v}\ \big|\ \{X^v_{p,0}\}_{p,v},\ \{a_{p,0:T-1}, e_p\}_{p=1}^N,\ c\big),\quad v\in\{\text{pov},\text{pano}\},$$

where ap,t are the keyboard and camera controls of player p, ep is a fixed appearance attribute of the player's character, and c holds a scene description and the initial camera orientations. Future positions, visibility and images are never provided. The panorama is therefore both a supervised prediction target and a source of context for the local view.

Geometry-aligned dual towers

Each projection has its own video tower: the POV tower is initialized from Gamma-World and the panorama tower from PanoWorld. The same direction sits at different image coordinates in the two projections, and the POV camera turns with the head. Every token is therefore located by the world-frame ray it observes. Rotating each query and key by the rotary phases Φ(d) of its ray gives

$$\tilde{\mathbf q}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf k}=\mathbf q^{\top}\mathsf R\big(\Phi(\mathbf d^{\text{pano}})-\Phi(\mathbf d^{\text{pov}})\big)\mathbf k,$$

Tokens that observe the same direction have zero relative phase, whatever their image coordinates or head orientation. Each direction of the exchange adds its output through a zero-initialized channel gate, so training starts from the two pretrained towers.

Player identity encoding

Following Gamma-World, m rotary phase planes in self-attention encode player identity. Gamma-World's regular-simplex codes admit at most m+1 players. PAWA uses quadratic-phase codes from mutually unbiased bases, for an odd prime m:

$$\theta^{(a,b)}_r=\tfrac{2\pi}{m}(ar^2+br),\quad a,b,r\in\mathbb F_m,$$

giving m2 codes whose normalized inner products take only three values:

$$\big|\langle z^{(a,b)},z^{(a',b')}\rangle\big|\in\{1,\ 0,\ m^{-1/2}\}.$$

After reserving the all-zero code, players are assigned from m2−1 codes, 120 in our configuration. Codes are resampled for every training example and permuted together with observations, controls and appearance, so the model distinguishes players by relative codes rather than fixed indices.

Training objective

Both towers are trained jointly with flow matching, with one noise level shared by the two projections. Co-players often fill a small part of the frame. Projected body boxes of the other players therefore raise the loss weight on co-player regions, while each sample keeps a unit mean weight.

Causal generation and distillation

The towers are first adapted to their projections, then joined into a bidirectional teacher. A block-causal generator with diffusion forcing and Gamma-World's sparse hub attention gives PAWA (Causal), sampled with 35 steps. Self Forcing with distribution matching distillation gives PAWA (Distilled), with four steps per causal block. Both are trained on 2, 4, 8 and 16 players.

Comparison with multiplayer world models

The baselines are the bidirectional, causal and distilled variants of Gamma-World, Solaris, and MultiWorld, which runs its official 50-step autoregressive sampler with Minecraft controls mapped to its action space. Each baseline receives the initial POV observations. PAWA additionally receives the initial panoramas defined by its task. The main comparison uses 30 two-player interaction episodes of 12 s at 20 Hz, absent from the final training split.

Every model plays the same episode, synchronized frame by frame. External models have no panoramic output, so the last tile shows the ground-truth panorama above PAWA's prediction. A–E are the episodes above; A is the case of the paper's two-player comparison figures. F is an interaction-tier episode with mutual visibility; it is one of the six development episodes used in checkpoint selection. G has the largest per-episode increase in POV LPIPS from distillation. Tiles keep each model's native field of view and aspect ratio; resolutions, preprocessing and action interfaces differ across models.

PAWA (Causal) achieves the best POV scores on four of the five metrics and PAWA (Distilled) the best FID, so both variants surpass every baseline on every POV metric. Against the strongest baseline, Gamma-World (Bidirectional), PAWA (Causal) lowers POV FVD from 267.1 to 120.1 and LPIPS from 0.567 to 0.421, and raises PSNR from 13.08 to 16.86 dB. With the same four-step budget as Gamma-World (Distilled), PAWA (Distilled) reaches POV FVD 138.1 against 279.7. The panoramic predictions reach LPIPS 0.223 and 20.41 dB PSNR for the causal model and stay close for the distilled one.

Scaling the player population

The evaluated Gamma-World configuration provides at most four player codes, whereas the quadratic-phase codebook of PAWA assigns a distinct identity to every player of larger sessions. With four players, PAWA (Causal) reduces POV FVD from 323.6 for the best Gamma-World variant to 138.0 and outperforms it on every POV metric. With eight and sixteen players, which only PAWA supports among the evaluated models, the distilled model keeps POV FVD between 116.7 and 167.3, within the range of the two-player results, and its LPIPS rises only moderately.

Analysis of the cross-tower exchange

The POV prediction draws on the panorama

Disabling all seven cross-tower sites of PAWA (Distilled) at inference, with fixed weights, episodes, recorded noise and sampler, raises POV FVD from 138.1 to 179.0 and LPIPS from 0.465 to 0.510, while the panoramic scores change little. Removing only the pano→POV direction accounts for the entire loss, and early and late sites contribute comparably.

Limitations and scope

GPU memory and training budgets limit the clip length and exposure of the largest populations, so our evaluation extends to sixteen players. The pipeline already records more than 64 players and the codebook provides 120 identities; scaling training and evaluation toward these populations is a direct next step. Over long rollouts, co-player regions lose fidelity, a limitation shared by all evaluated multiplayer world models. The POV and panoramic forecasts of PAWA also gradually diverge, because the exchange aligns directions without enforcing pixel agreement.
